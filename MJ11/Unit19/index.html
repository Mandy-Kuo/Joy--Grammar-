Today’s mission

Compare with Care!

We will compare how people do things—and write kind notes to our teachers.

Who works hard? Who works harder? Who works the hardest?

Concept

Three ways to compare

the same
as + adjective/adverb + as
two people
-er / more … + than
three or more
the -est / the most …
Teacher model

Same: as … as

A + verb + as + adverb + as + B

The princess dances as well as the queen.

Use the original form: well—not better or best.

Teacher model

Two: comparative + than

short word
Ivy hits the ball harder than Leo.
long adverb
Patricia works more quickly than Oliver.
question
Did Tina arrive later than you?
Teacher model

Three or more: the superlative

the + -est / most + adjective or adverb

Samuel came to school the earliest of all the students.

Sandy jumps the highest in our school.

Guided choice

Choose the correct answer.

The last story is _____ than the others.

Two things are compared, so use more exciting than.

Guided choice

Choose the correct answer.

Jenny sang more _____ than her classmates.

more confidently

Combine

Put the ideas together.

The queen dances well.
The princess dances well, too.
Use “as … as”

The princess dances as well as the queen.

Unscramble

Make a sentence.

his sister / Oliver / more beautifully / dances / than

Oliver dances more beautifully than his sister.

Note clues

Who gets the note? Who writes it?

Dear / Hi / Hello
The name after the greeting is the reader.

Dear Miss Thomas,
→ The note is for Miss Thomas.
Your student
The name at the bottom is the writer.

Your student,
Amy
→ Amy writes the note.
Remember
Top name = gets it

Bottom name = writes it
Writing model

A short thank-you note

Dear Miss Thomas,

Thank you for teaching me.
Your classes are interesting.
I am more confident now.
You are a great teacher.

Your student,
Amy

Change “Miss Thomas” to your teacher’s name. Change “Amy” to your own name.

Read a note
Hi, Bob,

You made lots of mistakes in your science report. Please correct them. Be careful when you do it. I’ll be back at 1 p.m.

Mrs. Price
1. Who is the note for? It is for Bob.
2. Who wrote the note? Mrs. Price wrote it.
Read a note
Hi, Bob,

Please correct the mistakes in your science report. Be careful when you do it.

Mrs. Price
What does Mrs. Price want Bob to do?

She wants him to correct his report carefully.
